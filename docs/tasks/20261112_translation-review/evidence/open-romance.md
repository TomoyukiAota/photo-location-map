# Left open after the review: Spanish, Catalan, Galician, Basque, French, Italian, Portuguese (Brazil), Portuguese (Portugal)

From the report of round 7, the last one, for these eight languages: what the reviewers could not
confirm, what they left because two wordings are equally good, and what is the owner's to decide. A speaker
of one of these languages can settle most of it at a glance. The decisions that were made, with their
sources, are in `decisions-romance.md`.

## Equally good, left

New in this round (seen as a fresh reader, weighed, and left because neither side is clearly better):

- **`fr`, `welcome.usageInfoCollection`**: `Pour être améliorée, cette application collecte des données
  d’utilisation anonymes.` The first pass called it "faithful and correct, a little stiff" and left it; no later
  round weighed it. It is the only one of the eight that keeps "this application collects" as an active clause:
  the other seven, and the Japanese, say that the data "is collected", and so does Photo Data Viewer's French
  `about.usageData` for the same opening (`Pour améliorer cette application, des données d’utilisation sont
  collectées`). The two French constructions each give something up: the application's is strictly grammatical
  (the infinitive has the subject of the main verb) and reads like a translation; Photo Data Viewer's is how such
  notices are usually written and leaves the infinitive without its subject. No source settles which to prefer,
  and the two sentences are not a name that a user must recognize in both places. Left as they are. If a French
  speaker reviews the files, this is the first sentence to show them.
- **`pt-BR`, `settings.cache.thereforeCached`**: `ficam guardadas no cache` (plain words) against `são
  armazenadas em cache`, the phrase of Chrome's Brazilian Portuguese (from knowledge of Chrome; not looked up).
  Both are understood at once; the text is an explanation for anyone who has photos, not a label.
- **`ca`, "remove" and "delete"**: `Elimina la regió` (`map.removeRegion`) beside `Suprimeix` (`settings.os.remove`,
  `settings.cache.delete`). Catalan software has both verbs for both English words (Firefox `Elimina` for
  "Remove" and `Suprimeix` for "Delete"; Google's Catalan `Suprimeix` for both; from knowledge of them). They are
  three different things (a region on the map, an item of Explorer's menu, the cache), each worded the same
  wherever it comes up, so nothing was made the same. Round 4 left `Suprimeix` beside macOS's `Elimina` for the
  cache.
- **`gl`, Photo Data Viewer's `demo.description`**: `Ao premer o botón` ("on pressing the button") where the
  other Galician texts say `Fai clic`. The page is used on phones too, where a button is pressed; Basque has the
  same (`botoia sakatzean`).

Everything under "Equally good, left" in the reports of rounds 3 to 6 was read again and stays there.

## Still not confirmed

The same as after rounds 5 and 6. This round's searches found nothing for them:

- **Explorer's label for "Date taken" in `pt-PT`, `gl` and `eu`** (`Data da captura`, `Data de captura`,
  `Ateratze-data`). Searched: the two candidate labels of Portugal with Explorer's Portuguese name, on the open web
  and on Microsoft's sites alone; two articles of a Portuguese technology site (pplware.sapo.pt) that the search
  found, neither of which names the column; a Galician query, which found only Spanish and Portuguese pages.
- **SmartScreen's two buttons in `eu`** (`Informazio gehiago`, `Exekutatu hala ere`): one search for the dialog's
  likely Basque title with the button names, nothing (twelve searches over four rounds). In `es`, `ca`, `gl`,
  `pt-BR` and `pt-PT` they are quoted by third parties only.

Both need a Windows in the language. Another reading or another search of this kind will not settle them: I
suggest that later rounds do not spend searches on them again.
